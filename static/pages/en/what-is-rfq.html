What is an RFQ? How to prepare a freight price request

An RFQ (Request for Quotation) is a document in which a buyer asks several suppliers for a price under the same conditions. In freight it lets you compare carriers' offers fairly.

RFQ, RFP and RFI

  • RFI (Request for Information): asks about the market and capabilities.
  • RFQ (Request for Quotation): asks for a price against precise requirements. This is what is mostly used in freight.
  • RFP (Request for Proposal): asks for a solution and a price, for more complex projects.

Why use an RFQ in freight

  • Comparison: all carriers price the same information.
  • Transparency: the basis of the decision is documented.
  • Time saving: the same question is not answered again and again.
  • Market knowledge: you see what a normal price level is.

What a good RFQ contains

  • Loading and unloading place (port or airport for sea and air)
  • Cargo description, weight, volume or number of pallets
  • Transport mode and container type
  • Incoterm with a named place
  • Dates: loading and delivery
  • Special requirements: temperature, dangerous goods, dimensions
  • Costs the price should include
  • Quote deadline

Example:

Loading: Ningbo port, China
Unloading: Alat port, Azerbaijan
Cargo: car spare parts (HS 8708), in cartons
Weight / volume: 18,400 kg / 52 CBM, 2 × 40HC containers
Incoterm: FOB Ningbo
Ready: 20.11.2026, delivery by 31.12.2026 at the latest
Price should include: terminal charges, container release
Quote deadline: 14.11.2026, 17:00

How to compare offers

Beyond the price, look at what the price covers, transit time, offer validity, payment terms, currency and the carrier's reliability. The cheapest offer is not always the best: hidden costs can appear later.

Frequently asked questions

RFQ or just asking for a price: what is the difference?

An RFQ is structured and sent to everyone under the same conditions, so the offers can be compared directly.

What is the deadline in an RFQ for?

Carriers know by when to reply and you can decide on time.

How many carriers should I ask?

There is no exact number, but several offers (usually three or more) show the market level.
